Session 1: Exodus and the Exodus

Key Takeaways
The exodus event establishes and helps define the identity of the community.
Pay attention to who is named and who is not. The biblical authors illustrate the priorities of the text
with details like these.
Moses’ deliverance and commissioning sets the pattern for later events in the book.

Did the Events in Exodus Really Happen?
“As a constitutive event, the exodus is recognized as an event of such import that the community would not
be what it is without its having occurred.”
Fretheim, Terence (2010). Exodus (Interpretation: A Bible Commentary for Teaching and Preaching).
Westminster John Knox Press.
See: Deuteronomy 26:1-9; Joshua 24:1-28; Isaiah 43:14-21; Psalm 105

When Did the Exodus Happen?
A summary of Nahum Sarna’s Exodus from the Jewish Publication Society.

15th Century
The argument for dating the exodus to the 15th century is largely based on 1 Kings 6:1, which counts 480
years from the exodus to Solomon’s reign. Major challenges to this date arise in that Pharaoh lived at Thebes
during that time, 400 miles away, and controlled Canaan.
Perhaps this number is somewhat symbolic, representing 12 generations at 40 years per generation, when in
reality the number is considerably smaller.

13th Century
Based on the location of the storage cities being built in Exodus 1:11, some date the exodus to the 13th
century when Pharaoh lived near Pithom and Rameses. At this time, Egypt had lost control of Canaan, and the
archaeological record attests to the existence of “Israel” as mentioned on Pharaoh Merneptah’s Stele (c.122411).
But what about the 480 years? Furthermore, geography and archaeology of the conquest don’t match
exactly.

3-4: Moses commissioned by YHWH
Exodus 5-40: The Saga of the Hebrews
5-18: Hebrews delivered from Pharaoh
5-11: Ten plagues
12-15a: Passover and exodus
15b-18: Wilderness journey
19-40: Hebrews commissioned by YHWH
19-24: Covenant
25-40: Presence

Sessão 21: O Nascimento de IsaqueSession 21: Isaac’s Birth

Principais LiçõesKey Takeaways

  • Os autores bíblicos usam design literário e palavras-chave de ligação para transmitir tanto a alegria de Sara quanto seu eco de dúvida no nascimento de Yitskhaq.The biblical authors use literary design and key linking words to both convey Sarah’s joy and echo her doubt at the birth of Yitskhaq.
  • Ao pensar em como abordar essas histórias, a visão macro é tão importante quanto a micro.As we think about how to approach these stories, the macro view is just as important to consider as the micro view.
  • Quando os parceiros escolhidos de Deus se comportam injustamente, Deus deve trabalhar em circunstâncias comprometidas para realizar seus propósitos com e através desses parceiros.When God’s chosen partners behave unjustly, God must work in compromised circumstances to accomplish his purposes with and through these partners.

A Promessa de Deus Cumprida e a Crível Tênue de SaraGod’s Promise Fulfilled and Sarah’s Tenuous Belief

O design de três passos deste parágrafo contrasta as ações de Iavé e de Avraham com a resposta de Sara. Observe a ênfase em cada subunidade na palavra falada de Iavé que prometeu o nascimento de um filho: “… como Iavé disse … como Iavé falou … como Elohim ordenou.” Essa ênfase no cumprimento da promessa nos faz avaliar as palavras de Sara mais profundamente. Sua pergunta “Quem teria dito a Avraham que Sara amamentaria filhos?” é facilmente respondida: “Iavé disse a vocês ambos, lá em Gênesis 18!” Em outras palavras, a resposta de Sara é primeiro grata (“riso”), mas a palavra-chave “rir” nos lembra o riso de Avraham que demonstrou sua falta de confiança em Iavé em Gênesis 17:17. O riso de Sara pode ser entendido de dois modos: celebração da generosidade de Deus, mas também, como o de Avraham, resposta de incredulidade — sua pergunta “Quem teria dito…” parece mais do que retórica.The three-step design of this paragraph contrasts Yahweh’s actions and Avraham’s actions with Sarah’s response. Notice the emphasis in each sub-unit on the spoken word of Yahweh that promised the birth of a son, “… just as Yahweh said … just as Yahweh spoke … just as Elohim commanded.” This emphasis on Yahweh’s fulfillment of the promise makes us evaluate Sarah’s words in a deeper way. Her question, “Who would have told Avraham that Sarah would nurse sons?” is easily answered: “Yahweh told you both, back in Genesis chapter 18!” In other words, Sarah’s response is first grateful (“laughter”), but the key word “laugh” reminds us of Avraham’s laughter that demonstrated his lack of trust in Yahweh back in Genesis 17:17. Sarah’s laughter can be understood in two ways: celebration of God’s generosity, but also, like Avraham, a response of unbelief.

Yitskhaq, Circuncisão e Ecos da Obediência de NoéYitskhaq, Circumcision, and Echoes of Noah’s Obedience

Esta unidade está repleta de alusões à narrativa do dilúvio e a Gênesis 17. Avraham é obediente ao mandamento divino dado em Gênesis 17:9-14 de que todos em sua família devem ser circuncidados como sinal da aliança de Deus com eles. Assim como Noé foi justo e obediente, Avraham aqui age em alinhamento com a vontade de Deus — um contraste com seu engano anterior.This unit is riddled with allusions to the flood narrative and to Genesis 17, an earlier Avraham narrative that also echoed the flood narrative. Avraham is obedient to the divine command given in Genesis 17:9-14 that everyone in his family should be circumcised as a sign of God’s covenant with them. Just as Noah was righteous and obedient, Avraham here acts in alignment with God’s will — a contrast with his earlier deception.

Questão para ReflexãoReflection Question

Como o nascimento de Isaque cumpre a promessa de Deus e, ao mesmo tempo, revela a fé ainda titubeante de Sara?How does Isaac’s birth fulfill God’s promise while also revealing Sarah’s still-hesitant faith?

Look, today you have banished )‫ (גרש‬me from the face of the ground. In Genesis 3, the woman does what is good in her eyes and her husband participates. In Genesis 16, Sarah and Avraham do the same. In Genesis 21 however, the situation is more complex, because God has committed himself to the lineage born through Sarah and not through Hagar. He will still bless and provide for Hagar’s son, but Yitskhaq is the chosen son. God acquiesces to Sarah’s selfish behavior, telling Avraham to “listen to her voice.” In both Genesis 3 and 16, the man listening to the voice of his wife is a negative thing. How should it be evaluated here? Now God commits to both Avraham and Sarah but also to Hagar, based on the divine promise to bless her son as well (Gen. 16:10). It’s as if God knows that Sarah will not tolerate having a rival wife and son around, and, in the interests of protecting Hagar and Yishmael, God allows them to be expelled. The fact that this is immediately followed by God’s rescue of Hagar and Yishmael shows that he has their well-being in mind, even if it means a painful separation. This itself finds a certain analogy with the Eden narrative, as God expels the very humans that he wanted to bless. And so even the act of separation includes a hidden promise of blessing that focuses on the seed (Gen. 3:15 and 21:13). What does this episode tell us about Avraham, about Sarah, and about God?

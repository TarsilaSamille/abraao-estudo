Sessão 19: Um Dilúvio de FogoSession 19: A Flood of Fire

Principais LiçõesKey Takeaways

  • Os anjos de Deus livram Ló da destruição, mas Ló ainda quer escolher sua própria versão de segurança.God’s angels deliver Lot from destruction, but Lot still wants to choose his own version of safety.
  • Autores bíblicos posteriores invocam Sodoma e Gomorra como ícones do juízo rápido e total de Deus sobre a injustiça, imoralidade e violência humanas.Later biblical authors invoke Sodom and Gomorrah as icons of God’s swift and total judgment on human injustice, immorality, and violence.
  • O episódio com Ló e suas filhas é a história de origem das nações moabita e amonita.The episode with Lot and his daughters is the origin story of the Moabite and Ammonite nations.

O Livramento de Ló e o Livramento de NoéLot’s Escape and Noah’s Deliverance

O livramento de Ló da cidade é descrito em linguagem desenhada para recordar o livramento de Noé das águas do dilúvio. “Toma tua mulher e tuas duas filhas” (19:15) ecoa “Entrarás na arca, tu e teus filhos e tua mulher” (6:18); “o fizeram descansar fora da cidade” (19:16) ecoa “a arca descansou sobre os montes de Ararat” (8:4); “achaste graça” (19:19) ecoa “Noé achou graça” (6:8); e “Iavé se lembrou de Avraham” (19:29) ecoa “Elohim se lembrou de Noé” (8:1). Embora seja Avraham quem é verdadeiramente como Noé, sua intercessão resulta em Ló sendo “mantido vivo” no dilúvio do juízo. A narrativa mostra que a intercessão do justo é eficaz em trazer o livramento dos aflitos.Lot’s deliverance from the city is described in language designed to recall Noah’s deliverance from the waters of the flood. “Take your wife and your two daughters” (19:15) echoes “You will enter the ark, you and your sons and your wife” (6:18); “they rested him outside the city” (19:16) echoes “the ark rested on the mountains of Ararat” (8:4); “you have found favor” (19:19) echoes “Noah found favor” (6:8); and “Yahweh remembered Avraham” (19:29) echoes “Elohim remembered Noah” (8:1). Even though it’s Avraham who is truly like Noah, his intercession results in Lot being “kept alive” in the flood of judgment. The narrative is showing that the intercession of the righteous one is effective in bringing about the deliverance of the afflicted.

O Livramento de Ló … Com RessalvasLot’s Deliverance … With Qualifications

Com a história de Ló, somos apresentados a uma nova complicação em seu caráter — ele não quer o livramento que Iavé oferece, pelo menos não inteiramente. Através de uma elaborada série de trocadilhos, o narrador retrata Ló como caprichoso e mesquinho no momento de sua salvação. Em vez de aceitar o refúgio que Deus providenciou, Ló quer negociar sua própria salvação, para que o resgate aconteça em seus próprios termos. Isso se assemelha à negociação de Caim sobre o livramento que Iavé provê após o assassinato de Abel (Gn 4:14-17): ambos rejeitam a provisão de Iavé e provêm sua própria forma de livramento através de uma cidade (עיר).With the story of Lot, we are introduced to a new complication in his character—he doesn’t want the deliverance that Yahweh offers, at least not entirely. Through an elaborate series of wordplays, the narrator portrays Lot as fickle and petty in the moment of his salvation. Instead of accepting the refuge that God has provided, Lot wants to negotiate his own salvation, so the rescue happens on his own terms. This bears an important resemblance to Cain’s negotiation over the deliverance that Yahweh provides after the murder of Abel (Gen. 4:14-17): both reject the provision of Yahweh and instead provide their own form of deliverance through a city (עיר).

GEN 18:1-19
The opening and closing stories of Genesis chapters 18-19 are about the survival of the future seed. After the flood of judgment and mercy on Avraham in Genesis 17, God’s promise of a future seed/son remains in Genesis 18:1-15. The promise is given in two Eden spots, under a tree and inside a tent. The closing story of Lot’s deliverance from the flood of judgment on Sodom is also about the survival of the future seed. But here, the daughters turn their legitimate concern into a distorted scheme of incest: They will deceive their father with wine in order to keep seed alive (Gen. 19:32, 34). Noah and Ham, Lot and His Daughters The significance of the story of Lot and his daughters is also illuminated by its placement as a hinge piece between the Sodom story and the next part of the Avraham story. The narrative about Lot’s deliverance from Sodom because of Avraham’s intercession came to a close in 19:27-29, as Avraham looks down. The story in Genesis 19:30-38, while about Lot, actually stands as a distinct episode after the ruin of Sodom. In this way, the story is precisely aligned with the story of Noah and Ham after the flood.

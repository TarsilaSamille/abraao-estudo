Class Notes: Joseph 205 of 215 Session 27: Jacob’s Burial Key Takeaways Yoseph goes up to Canaan, which contrasts with his going down to Egypt, highlighting the hope of a return to Eden threaded throughout the story of Genesis. The Egyptians embalm and mourn for Yaaqov, united with Yoseph's family in grief, showing how the blessed family of Abraham is sown among the nations. Yoseph imitates God as he comforts his brothers with the gift of reconciliation, demonstrating trust in God’s faithfulness. In Genesis 50:19-21, Yoseph summarizes themes from the entire book of Genesis regarding God’s plan to bring good out of evil and provide comfort to a world suffering under the consequences of sin. Paragraph 2: Genesis 50:1-11 1 And Yoseph fell upon the face of his father, and he wept over him, and he kissed him. 2 And Yoseph commanded his servants the healers to embalm his father, and the healers embalmed Yisra’el. 3 And they fulfilled for him forty days, because this is how the days of the embalmers were fulfilled. Then Egypt wept for him for seventy days. 4-5 And Yoseph said to the house of Phar’oh, “My father made me swear an oath, saying, ‘In my burial-grave which I dug for myself in the land of Cana’an, bury me there.’ And now, may I go up, so I can bury my father.” Genesis 50:1-11. Translation and Literary Design by Tim Mackie for BibleProject Classroom: Joseph (2022).

After Yaaqov’s death, Yoseph goes up to Canaan to bury his father — the reversal of the “going down” to Egypt that opened the story, and a sign of the hope of return to the land and to Eden. The Egyptians join Yoseph’s family in embalming and mourning Yaaqov for seventy days, showing how Abraham’s blessed family is now sown among the nations. When the brothers later fear retaliation, Yoseph comforts them and imitates God’s own character: “Do not fear, for am I in the place of God? As for you, you meant evil against me, but God meant it for good” (Gen 50:19-20). With that, the book of Genesis closes not in vengeance but in reconciliation.

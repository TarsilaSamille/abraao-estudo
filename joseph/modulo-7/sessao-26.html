Gathered With the Ancestors in the Cave of Makpelah In this story, Yaaqov approaches his own death and requests that he be buried in the land of his ancestors in a specific cave in the field of Makpelah. Death, Paradise, and the Passing of the Righteous The story of Sarah’s burial is a full-scale replay of the core themes at work in the garden of Eden story. Through wordplays, hyperlinks, and narrative analogies, Abraham’s loss of Sarah is compared to both the loss of eternal life in Eden and to the hope of God’s promise to restore life. Cada uma das palavras “cova” e “Makpelah” tem ricas associações de trocadilho com a história do Éden. “Cova” (me‘arah / מערה) em hebraico significa literalmente “lugar de exposição/nudez”. Este substantivo hebraico está relacionado a duas raízes escritas com letras semelhantes, ambas significando “ser exposto, nu”: 1. ערר: ‘arar, ver Isaías 32:11. 2. עור: ‘ur, ver Habacuque 3:9, onde é grafado com as mesmas letras de “nudez” em Gênesis 2:25.Each of the words “cave” and “Makpelah” have rich wordplay associations with the Eden story. “Cave” (me‘arah / מערה) in Hebrew literally means “place of exposure/nakedness”. This Hebrew noun is related to two root words spelled with similar letters that both mean “to be exposed, naked” 1. ערר: ‘arar, see Isaiah 32:11. 2. עור: ‘ur, see Habakkuk 3:9, where it is spelled with the same letters as “nakedness” from Genesis 2:25.

“Makpelah” é um nome de lugar estranho que significa “dobra” ou “par”. A raiz é kaphal )(כפל, que significa “ser dobrado” ou “emparelhado” (ver exemplos do verbo em Êxodo 26:9; Ezequiel 21:19). O significado de Makpelah como “par” ou “dobro” é atestado cedo na história da interpretação judaica, pois os tradutores gregos da Septuaginta renderam esta palavra por διπλους, “gêmeo” ou “emparelhado”. Quando essas palavras curiosas se combinam, o nome desse lugar é “A Nudez do Par”. Esta é uma referência inteligente de volta ao momento em que Adão e Eva violam o mandamento de Deus, comem do fruto proibido e então percebem que “os dois estão nus” (Gn 2:24-25 e 3:6-7).“Makpelah” is an odd place name that means “doubling” or “pair.” The root is kaphal )(כפל, which means “to be doubled” or “paired” (see examples of the verb in Exod. 26:9; Ezek. 21:19). The meaning of Makpelah as “pair” or “double” is attested early in the history of Jewish interpretation, as the Greek Septuagint translators rendered this word with διπλους, “twin” or “paired.” When these curious words are combined, the name of this place is "The Nakedness of the Pair." This is a clever reference back to the moment when Adam and Eve violate God’s command, eat from the forbidden fruit, and then realize that “the two of them are naked” (Gen. 2:24-25 and 3:6-7).

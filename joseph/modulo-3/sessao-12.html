Class Notes: Joseph 74 of 215 Session 12: Joseph the Wise Deliverer of Egypt Key Takeaways Yoseph is lifted out of the pit, shaved, and given a whole new set of garments. In the Bible, shaving often marks a transition between two phases of life or states of being. The doubling in Pharaoh’s dream points to one reality confirmed by God, giving us further insight into the pattern of doubling in the Yoseph story as a whole. Yoseph is presented as a new Noah, with Pharaoh’s house as the ark where others will find shelter and provision. Genesis 41:1-36. Translation and Literary Design by Tim Mackie for BibleProject Classroom: Joseph (2022). Pharaoh’s two dreams — seven good cows devoured by seven ugly ones, seven good sheaves swallowed by seven thin ones — are a doubled sign pointing to a single reality: seven years of plenty followed by seven years of famine. When the cup-bearer finally remembers Yoseph and he is brought from the pit, shaved and re-clothed, he interprets the dreams and proposes a plan to store grain. Yoseph is thus elevated as a wise deliverer, presented as a new Noah whose house (like the ark) becomes the shelter where Egypt and the surrounding nations find life through the famine.

O Design SimétricoThe Symmetrical Design
  • Nas partes externas (Gn 45:1-3 e 45:14-25a), o choro de Yoseph é destacado, bem como o som alto do pranto que pode ser ouvido por todo o palácio real. Essas seções externas também mostram os irmãos em estado de choque (נבהל, Gn 45:3) e agitação (רגז, Gn 45:24). Essas duas palavras são frequentemente usadas como sinônimos para pessoas em estado de choque, medo e ira quando vivenciam eventos traumáticos (ver Êx 15:14; Jl 2:1).In the outer parts (Gen. 45:1-3 and 45:14-25a), Yoseph's weeping is highlighted, as well as the loud sound of crying that can be heard all throughout the royal palace. These outer sections also show the brothers in a state of shock (נבהל, Gen. 45:3) and agitation (רגז, Gen. 45:24). These two words are often used as synonyms for people in a state of shock, fear, and anger when they experience traumatic events (see Exod. 15:14; Joel 2:1).
  • Na parte central (Gn 45:4-13), Yoseph adverte seus irmãos duas vezes sobre os cinco anos restantes de fome (esses avisos formam uma moldura literária ao redor do versículo 8), e ele lhes diz como Elohim transformou a traição dos irmãos em um resgate, enviando-o ao Egito para se tornar governante de todos.In the center part (Gen. 45:4-13), Yoseph twice warns his brothers about the five remaining years of famine (these warnings form a literary frame around verse 8), and he tells them how Elohim turned the brother's treachery into a rescue by sending him to Egypt to become ruler of all.

"Each of these sibling rivalries or 'seed conflicts' develops the paradigmatic conflict first announced in Genesis 3:15 and then portrayed in Cain's murder of Abel. While only Genesis 4 ends in murder, the threat of fratricide against the covenant seed looms throughout each of these conflicts. The seed conflict between Joseph and his brothers is, thus, climactic in Genesis, as the brothers' intense animosity for Joseph evokes … Genesis 4. … The dramatic twist in the Joseph story is that Joseph's wise plan to 'test' his brothers (42:15-16), coupled with Judah's repentance (44:18-34), lead to a reversal of the Cain and Abel story. … The unresolved brotherly strife of previous generations is finally overcome by Yoseph's ruse in chapters 42-45, and by his refusal to exact vengeance on his brothers after Jacob's death (50:15-21). In this way, the Joseph story reverses one of the primary literary motifs in Genesis. Sibling conflict has racked the covenant family and endangered the seed since Adam and Eve's first children. Joseph undoes the cycle of violence, but only by exercising forgiveness after his own humiliation and exaltation. In this way, Joseph is an anti-Cain. He is truly his brothers' keeper." O Éden-Arca do EgitoThe Ark-Eden of Egypt

Recordai como o parágrafo acima do discurso de Yoseph (Gn 45:4-13) o retratou como um novo remanescente semelhante a Noé, que foi escolhido dentre a geração da fome para preservar a vida para um remanescente que sobreviveria através da des-criação. Aqui neste parágrafo, essa analogia continua.Recall how the above paragraph of Yoseph's speech (Gen. 45:4-13) portrayed him as a new Noah-like remnant who was chosen out of the generation of the famine to preserve life for a remnant that would survive through the de-creation. Here in this paragraph, that analogy continues.

Faraó começa a ordenar Yoseph e os irmãos a "ir" (בוא) de volta à sua terra e a "tomar" (לקח) seu pai e animais e "entrar" (בוא אל) no Egito, onde possam ter comida (אכל) e desfrutar do melhor da terra. Toda essa linguagem é um hiperlink de volta ao discurso de Deus a Noé antes do dilúvio.Pharaoh begins commanding Yoseph and the brothers to "go" (בוא) back to their land and to "take" (לקח) their father and animals and "come into" (בוא אל) Egypt, where they can have food (אכל) and enjoy the good of the land. All of this language is a hyperlink back to God's speech to Noah before the flood.

O Refúgio-ArcaThe Ark-Eden
  • Um evento de des-criação vem sobre a terra, tirando a vida.A de-creation event comes upon the land, taking away life.
  • Deus escolhe um líder justo (Yoseph // Noé) para criar um lugar de refúgio (o "bem" do Egito // a arca) onde sua família pode ser liberta.God chooses one righteous leader (Yoseph // Noah) to create a place of refuge (the "good" of Egypt // the ark) where their family can be delivered.
  • Os escolhidos levam muitos animais para o refúgio onde podem viver. Notai que a palavra "carros" (עגלות) é literalmente a palavra hebraica "bezerros" (עגל), referindo-se a um carro puxado por bois.The chosen ones take many animals into the refuge where they can live. Notice that the word "carts" (עגלות) is literally the Hebrew word "calves" (עגל), referring to a cart drawn by oxen.
  • Os escolhidos obedecem às ordens.The chosen ones obey the commands.

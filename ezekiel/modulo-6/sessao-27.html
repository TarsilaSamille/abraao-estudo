As Três Zonas Sagradas de Ezequiel 40-48The Three Sacred Zones of Ezekiel 40-48

Os nove capítulos de Ezequiel 40-48 avançam em uma sequência que lida com três espaços sagrados, ampliando-se a cada seção: (1) o templo e seu lugar na cidade; (2) a porção ao lado do templo e da cidade; (3) a terra ao redor do templo e da porção. A seção do templo (40:1-44:31) define os espaços dedicados ao templo e controla o acesso para prevenir abusos passados. A porção (45:1-46:24) separa o templo e o reino dos sacerdotes dos levitas e do rei, comunicando que o espaço de Yahweh não está sob o controle do sacerdócio, mas vice-versa. A terra (47:1-48:35) redistribui a terra prometida à organização das doze tribos, ignorando a topografia em favor do idealismo teológico.The nine chapters that make up Ezekiel 40-48 work forward in a sequence that deals with three sacred spaces, scaling larger with each section: (1) The temple and its place within the city; (2) The portion alongside the temple and city; (3) The land surrounding the temple and the portion. The Temple (Ezek. 40:1-44:31) defines the spaces dedicated to the temple and controls access to prevent past abuses. The Portion (Ezek. 45:1-46:24) separates the temple and the realm of the priests away from both the Levites and the king, communicating that Yahweh’s space is not under the control of the priesthood, but vice-versa. The Land (Ezek. 47:1-48:35) redistributes the promised land to the classic twelve tribe organization, disregarding geographic realism in favor of theological idealism.

O Propósito da Visão do TemploThe Purpose of Ezekiel's Temple Vision

Em Ezequiel 43:10-11, Ezequiel é mandado "declarar" a visão aos exilados, "para que possam medir a proporção". A palavra "proporção" é a chave: o raro substantivo hebraico toknit (תכנית) aparece apenas mais uma vez na Bíblia hebraica, em Ezequiel 28:12 ("selo de perfeita proporção"). Não significa "plano/planta" (tavnit), mas "medida perfeita, proporção ideal". As dimensões em sua maior parte horizontais mostram que o foco está na organização do espaço, não em uma estrutura a ser construída. O objetivo não é fornecer um projeto de construção, mas confrontar os exilados visualmente, expondo quão corrupto se tornou o templo de Jerusalém. Como disse Wright: "Ezequiel foi mandado a transmitir aos exilados tudo o que vira, especialmente as medidas simétricas, mas o propósito expresso daquela informação era induzir vergonha (43:10-11)... a visão de Ezequiel não era para fornecer orientação de como o templo deveria ser reconstruído, mas para dar a garantia da esperança de que seria reconstruído."In Ezekiel 43:10-11, Ezekiel is told to “declare” the vision to the exiles, “so that they can measure the proportion.” The word "proportion" is key: the rare Hebrew noun toknit (תכנית) appears only one other time in the Hebrew Bible, in Ezekiel 28:12 ("seal of perfect proportion"). It does not mean "plan/pattern" (tavnit), but "perfect measure, ideal proportion". The largely horizontal dimensions show the focus is on the arrangement of space, not a structure to be built. The purpose is not to provide a building plan, but to confront the exiles visually, exposing how corrupt the Jerusalem temple had become. As Wright puts it: “Ezekiel was told to convey to the exiles all that he had seen, especially the symmetrical measurements, but the express purpose of that information was to induce shame (43:10-11)... the purpose of Ezekiel’s vision was not to provide guidance as to how the temple was to be rebuilt, but to provide reassurance of the hope that it would be rebuilt.”

The Perfect Proportions of the Temple
What exactly are the “perfect proportions” of the temple? All of chapters 40-42 have been consumed on the
horizontal measurements of the temple complex. The lack of vertical dimensions (except for the outer wall
and the gate houses) shows that Ezekiel's vision focuses on the arrangement of space rather than on
structures as such.
“If one were to enter through the eastern and most important gate, one would observe a perfect
symmetrical design. What one sees to the north is perfectly mirrored by what one sees to the south, while
in contrast the views from the northern or southern gates would be asymmetrical ... Even more important
are the series of perfect squares in the compound. The entire temple complex is 500 by 500 cubits. The
most sacred area of all, the Holy of Holies is a square 20 cubits deep and 20 cubits wide ... The center of
the entire compound is the altar, whose upper platform measures 14 by 14 cubits while the base measures
20 by 20 cubits, matching the size of the Holy of Holies ... As one looked from the eastern gate, the
symmetrical design would lead the eyes up the center line up to the altar, and then past that up further
through the temple doors and into the most holy space ... The shape and size of the entire complex reflects
lofty theological and spiritual ideals. Its perfect design is in keeping with the perfection of the divine
resident.”

Vergonha e AutoconhecimentoShame and Self-Knowledge

Em contraste, Ezequiel não é mostrado ao templo ideal para lançar um novo programa de construção. Antes, a mensagem comunicada pela "proporção, desenho e forma" é uma continuação da crítica profética de Ezequiel à história de Israel. O desenho perfeito visa trazer seu auditório exilado à consciência de sua culpa, para que vejam como sua corrupção do templo levou à sua destruição e ao exílio. Para Ezequiel, há uma conexão crucial entre o autoconhecimento genuíno diante de Deus e a vergonha — a capacidade de experimentar vergonha constitui um ato salvífico de Yahweh, preparando o caminho para uma nova identidade moldada por um novo autoconhecimento.In stark contrast, Ezekiel is not shown the ideal temple in order to launch a new building program. Rather, the message communicated by the “proportion, design, and form” is a continuation of Ezekiel’s prophetic critique of Israel’s history. The perfect design is aimed at bringing his exilic audience to awareness of their guilt so they can see how their corruption of the temple led to its destruction and their isolation in exile. For Ezekiel, there is a crucial connection between genuine self-knowledge before God and shame — the capacity to experience shame constitutes a salvific act by Yahweh, paving the way for a new identity shaped by a new self-knowledge.
